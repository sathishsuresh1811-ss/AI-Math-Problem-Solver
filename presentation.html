Problem Statement

The Challenge of AI in Mathematical Education

The Problem

  • AI models frequently produce "hallucinated" arithmetic slips and formula misapplications.
  • A correct final answer can mask fundamentally invalid intermediate reasoning steps.
  • Confident language ("obviously", "clearly") often misleads students into accepting falsehoods.

Capstone Objectives

  • Construct a verified 30-problem benchmark across school/college curricula.
  • Automate exact mathematical scoring using SymPy (strictly without unsafe eval()).
  • Quantify whether self-verification prompts systematically correct reasoning errors.
Presenter Script: "The motivation behind our project is grounded in pedagogical safety. In mathematics, intermediate derivations matter just as much as final numbers. Our system enforces dual scoring: evaluating both terminal numerical equivalence and individual derivation steps."
Methodology

Curated 30-Problem Verified Benchmark

1. Algebra (10 Problems)

Linear equations, quadratic factoring, 2x2 systems, exponents, logarithmic domain restrictions, rational equations, infinite geometric series.

2. Probability & Stats (10)

Single event, independent dice, conditional probability, combinations, expected value, population variance, Bayes' theorem, binomial.

3. Calculus Basics (10)

Power rule, difference-of-squares limits, product rule, chain rule, definite/indefinite integrals, u-substitution, critical points, L'Hopital's rule.

Rigorous Standards: Every question features an independently verified canonical answer, documented mathematical formula, granular reference steps, and acceptable equivalent answer formats.
Presenter Script: "Slide 3 presents our dataset structure. We curated exactly 30 original problems distributed evenly across Algebra, Probability, and Calculus, spanning Easy, Medium, and Hard tiers. All solutions were independently verified with SymPy."
Prompt Engineering

Three Standardized Experimental Conditions

Condition A: Plain Zero-Shot Prompt

"Solve this mathematics problem: [problem]. Give your answer."

Condition B: Step-by-Step Reasoning Prompt

"Solve this mathematics problem step by step. State the relevant formula, explain each calculation, show intermediate steps, and provide the final answer..."

Condition C: Self-Verification Prompt

"...provide the final answer, independently verify the result using substitution, an alternative method, or another appropriate mathematical check, and correct any errors you identify."

Presenter Script: "Slide 4 shows our three experimental conditions. In condition A, we test raw intuition without guidance. In condition B, we enforce chain-of-thought derivations. In condition C, we test whether asking the model to verify via substitution catches calculation errors."

Critical Discovery: Verification does not guarantee correctness. In complex multi-branch questions (e.g. Bayes' Theorem or domain restrictions), models often suffer from verification confirmation bias, generating circular justifications for an initial slip.

Defense Conclusions

Conclusions, Limitations & Future Work

Key Takeaways

  • AI mathematical derivations require automated programmatic verification (SymPy/CAS).
  • Chain-of-thought prompting should be default for mathematical education.
  • Self-verification prompts should be paired with explicit external validation rather than pure reflection.

Future Roadmap

  • Integration with formal theorem provers (Lean 4 / Coq).
  • Multi-agent peer-review solvers (Solver + Critic + Verifier).
  • Expansion to Multivariable Calculus and Linear Algebra matrices.
